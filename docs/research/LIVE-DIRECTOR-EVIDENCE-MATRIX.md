# Live Director Evidence Matrix

**Owner:** Role 1 (`Dewflash`)

**Last updated:** 19 August 2026

**Method:** Secondary research and current-capability review only

**Status:** Evidence ledger complete for the current scope discussion; direct ChatXPT solution effect remains unproven

## Bottom line

The research supports a real but bounded problem: playing and producing an interactive livestream compete for attention, chat becomes harder to interpret as activity rises, and late joiners can lack context. It also shows that adjacent context and interaction systems can attract use.

It does **not** establish that ChatXPT's proposed Live Director loop reduces streamer effort, increases meaningful viewer participation, improves retention, or earns sustained adoption. ChatXPT is an evidence-informed product hypothesis. The first implementation priority, if accepted by the project owner, is the smallest experiment capable of proving or rejecting that hypothesis against existing Twitch and OBS workflows.

| Evidence level | Current conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| Pain exists | Supported for divided attention, audience-management cost, chat overload, and some late-join context difficulty; prevalence in ChatXPT's exact target segment is not quantified |
| Adjacent mechanisms can work | Supported in bounded studies for context aids, interactive Extensions, polls, and audience influence; results are mixed and often observational |
| ChatXPT's exact solution works | **Not established**; no reviewed study evaluates the complete game-state + audience-signal + safe sidequest + Twitch vote + OBS payoff loop |
| ChatXPT improves retention, growth, or revenue | **Not established** |
| ChatXPT is technically plausible | Partly supported by bounded gameplay-signal and Extension deployments; ChatXPT still needs its own runtime and comparative evidence |

## Master evidence-to-product table

The `How ChatXPT can serve` column is a product response inferred from the evidence. It is not a result reported by the cited source.

| Evidence | Quantitative result | What it supports | Limitation | How ChatXPT can serve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Wohn & Freeman, Audience Management Practices of Live Streamers, 2020](https://guof.people.clemson.edu/papers/imx20.pdf) | 25 semi-structured streamer interviews over four months; participants had 119-187,664 followers; ages 20-52; 11 women and 14 men | Streamers described attention trade-offs, missed chat, pausing play to catch up, moderator help, and cognitive cost from audience management | Qualitative, non-representative sample with a very wide channel-size range; no prevalence, causal effect, or ChatXPT test | Test one expiring private cue that compresses a relevant audience signal and waits for a tolerable gameplay moment; measure whether it reduces attention and actions versus manual monitoring |
| [Flores-Saviaga et al., Audience and Streamer Participation at Scale on Twitch, 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00215) | 44 collection days in 2017; 226,658 streams; 651,664 participating chatters; 12,150,866 messages; 2,700 minutes of analysed video. Reported clusters ranged from 0-6 average live viewers for `Clique` to 21,678+ for tournament/celebrity streams | Audience-management conditions differ sharply by operating mode; interaction timing changes with game intensity; bots and moderators are common; numbered chat choices already existed | Old platform data; cluster ranges are not a modern small/medium taxonomy; the reported day-one exit measure concerns chat participation across collection days, not within-stream viewer churn | Segment by current chat velocity, unique participants, moderation support, and gameplay phase instead of follower count; stay quiet in sparse chat and assist rather than replace moderators |
| [Nematzadeh et al., Information Overload in Group Communication, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6837236/) | In streams with at least 1,000 messages and 100 users (`N=43,969` streams), per-user participation peaked near 40 messages per five minutes (8/min) and declined above it; questions dropped sharply around 200 messages per five minutes (40/min); 50% of individually modelled users followed the expected inverted-U pattern | Chat load can shift conversation toward lower per-capita participation, shorter and more repetitive messages, fewer questions, and fewer direct interactions | Based on older aggregate Twitch chat; thresholds are descriptive, not universal product settings; it measures chat behaviour rather than streamer pain or cue value | Aggregate bursts into bounded, source-labelled pointers instead of cloning chat; calibrate thresholds on ChatXPT sessions and retain ambiguity rather than copying the published cut-offs |
| [Hamilton et al., Streaming on Twitch, 2014](https://ecologylab.net/research/publications/streamingOnTwitch.pdf) | Nearly four years of ethnographic observation plus interviews with 11 streamers and four viewers | Split attention, moderator/regular support, and meaningful participation in smaller communities are durable social mechanisms | Very early Twitch era; qualitative; does not measure present tooling or the value of automation | Preserve direct creator-viewer conversation in sparse communities and make automation optional, restrained, and streamer-controlled |
| [CatchLive, CHI 2022](https://jisuyim.com/figure/publication/catchlive.pdf) | 67 viewers across three deployments. The game comparison used 16 CatchLive viewers versus 17 baseline viewers for about 25 minutes. CatchLive produced 11.13 chat messages per viewer (`SD=8.43`) versus 1.53 (`SD=1.31`), `p<.01`, and 2.75 snapshots (`SD=.74`) versus 1.18 (`SD=.97`), `p<.01`. Self-reported engagement was 3.94 versus 3.82, `p=.34`; understanding was 3.84 versus 3.69, `p=.12`; prior-content understanding was 3.19 versus 2.53, `p=.09`; factual recall was not significant, `p=.44`. Nine of 16 game viewers said highlights helped the storyline. Game summaries were the most distracting of the three genres at 3.25/5 `not distracting` | This is the strongest reviewed adjacent treatment comparison: a context system changed participation proxies, while comprehension and self-reported engagement remained statistically inconclusive. It supports testing a minimal catch-up aid, not assuming it works | Small, short deployment/comparison and a feature-rich baseline; interaction counts are proxies, not retention; CatchLive is a timeline/highlight system, not ChatXPT | Treat Catch-up as a removable experiment: one current-state card, no timeline. Compare it with OBS plus vote-only, require comprehension improvement, and reject it if it distracts or slows voting |
| [Sjöblom & Hamari, Why Do People Watch Others Play Video Games?, 2017](https://www.utupub.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/e4a966b0-1fc6-44a8-8924-4dedf232babe/content) | 1,097 questionnaire responses, 1,091 retained. Social-integration motivation was associated with hours watched (`beta=.132`, `p<.01`), streamers watched (`beta=.120`, `p<.05`), channels followed (`beta=.213`, `p<.001`), and subscriptions (`beta=.150`, `p=.002`); tension release had the largest association with hours watched (`beta=.319`, `p<.001`) | Social connection and information are relevant motivations in game-stream viewing | Survey/structural-model associations, not experimental causality; older Twitch context; does not validate quests or a specific interface | Frame sidequests as optional community participation and keep the viewer path low-friction; do not translate these associations into retention or subscription promises |
| [Melhart et al., Towards a Comprehensive Model of Mediated Engagement in Video Games, 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07207) | More than 100,000 events from several hundred PUBG matches across five popular streamers and 40 telemetry features; reported individual average engagement-prediction accuracy around 76-80%, peaks up to 84%, and cross-style averages around 75.4-78% | Gameplay phase and event features can be informative enough to test phase-aware timing in a bounded title | One game, popular streamers, native telemetry rather than OBS vision, and chat frequency as an engagement proxy; no game-neutral accuracy claim | Use only fresh supported activity/quiet/transition evidence to suppress cues during high focus; expose confidence and `unknown`; never claim to know audience experience |
| [Twitch Creator Camp and Channel Points guidance](https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/establish-your-brand/engaging-viewers/) | Twitch reports first-visit chatters are 50% more likely to return, Channel Points can increase time spent by up to 18% and chat participation by up to 13%, and visible polls can increase engagement by 10% | Twitch considers participation strategically important and native primitives can correlate with or produce engagement | Vendor-reported; no disclosed samples, methods, controls, or uncertainty; cannot be used as an independent causal benchmark for ChatXPT | Use Twitch-native participation surfaces and compare ChatXPT directly with native polls/rewards; do not promise or set these percentages as ChatXPT targets |
| [Chitayat et al., From Passive Viewer to Active Fan, IMX 2024](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/211999/1/IMX_Weavr_Overlay.pdf) | Dota 2 Twitch Extension deployment across 60 matches and four weeks; approximately 306,000 exposed users, 101,729 direct users (about 33%), and 1,628,017 interactions. 68.5% of interactions occurred during live matches, 5.2% during draft, and 26.3% between matches; 15,302 users interacted at least 20 times. Authors classified 55% of active users as shallow and 20% as power users. The source contains a 306,545/306,645 total-user discrepancy | A Twitch Extension can attract optional use alongside live gameplay, and interaction follows match cadence; viewers vary greatly in desired depth | High-profile Dota esports context; information/recap Extension rather than voting or sidequests; observational telemetry; passive exposure and retention are not established | Keep the Extension optional and useful outside voting through personal receipt, active/result continuity, and a narrowly tested Catch-up state; do not force every viewer into the same depth |
| [Lessel et al., Let's Play My Way, 2017](https://umtl.cs.uni-saarland.de/paper_preprints/paper_lets_play_my_way_investigating_audience_influence_in_user_generated_gaming_livestreams.pdf) | Rocket Beans case: 24 polls averaging 9,533 votes (`SD=5,338`). Lab study: `n=8`, four roughly 15-minute conditions; perceived fun medians rose from 3.5 without gamification to 5.5-6 with gamification; Friedman `chi-square(3)=10.87`, `p=.012`, but Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise comparisons were not significant | Repeated audience influence and voting can support meaningful participation; context, moderation, and presentation matter | The large case is an established produced roleplay show with no control group; the lab is tiny and studies direct Pokemon control, not safe sidequests; no retention effect | Preserve bounded voting and visible follow-through, but keep streamer veto, context, and deterministic safety; evaluate the whole loop rather than citing poll volume as proof |
| [Lessel et al., Streamer's Hell, 2022](https://umtl.cs.uni-saarland.de/paper_preprints/Lessel-StreamersHell.pdf) | Online study with 81 viewers followed by a lab study with six streamers | Counterevidence: audience influence is not universally desirable; suitability depends on genre, context, customisation, and streamer control | Explores effects on the streamer's room, hardware, body, and environment rather than sidequests; small streamer lab and best-case setting | Make participation opt-in, game/context-aware, bounded by hard preferences, and immediately pausable; reject universal or autonomous intervention claims |
| [Schuck et al., Viewer Types in Game Live Streams, 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11257-022-09328-9) | Exploratory study retained 211 US viewers; validation study retained 148. The work produced a 25-item instrument with five viewer types; 37 of 38 feature preferences were significantly predictable by at least one viewer-type score. Model fit was mixed: `RMSEA=.077` met its criterion while `TLI=.855` did not | Viewers differ materially in whether they want to alter, observe, socialise around, or financially support streams; one interaction design should not be assumed universal | Crowdsourced US samples and hypothetical feature preferences; no in-the-wild ChatXPT behaviour; some validity measures were weak and authors call for larger samples | Preserve a passive broadcast experience, make the Extension opt-in, and avoid persistent viewer-personality profiling; evaluate participation by voluntary usage segments |
| [Seering et al., Proximate Social Factors in First-Time Contribution, CHI 2020](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2020_Newcomers.pdf) | 183 million Twitch messages; content comparison sampled 10,000 first-time and 10,000 established participants. First-time messages were about 25% shorter, first-timers asked questions 125% more often, and slightly over 15% of first messages were questions | Newcomers behave differently and can need context or response opportunities | Observational chat data; information-seeking first posts were negatively associated with short/medium retention, so questions are not automatically an opportunity; no game-state or product test | Offer private, public-safe current-state Catch-up without identifying or profiling newcomers; never treat a question or silence as consent or demand |
| [Arslan, When the Audience Joins the Game, 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1875952126000571) | 10,621 League of Legends ranked matches from Jan-Aug 2024: 4,921 non-streamer, 4,863 off-stream streamer, and 837 on-stream matches. Within streamers, on-stream games had fewer kills/deaths, higher KDA, more vision control, and no win-rate difference; adjusted models retained fewer deaths, more assists, higher KDA/vision, but not win probability | Broadcasting is associated with changes in gameplay behaviour, strengthening the case for low-distraction, phase-aware controls | Observational, one competitive game, no chat or engagement measure, and residual confounding remains possible | Suppress nonessential cues during high-focus play and measure distraction; do not infer that ChatXPT improves gameplay or that all genres respond alike |

## What each evidence class permits us to say

| Claim | Status | Permitted language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Streamers can face divided attention and audience-management cost | Supported, bounded | “Prior research documents the problem in qualitative and large-scale Twitch studies.” |
| Chat becomes harder to sustain as activity rises | Supported, bounded | “Aggregate Twitch research found nonlinear participation and conversation degradation at higher message loads.” |
| Late joiners can lack context | Supported, bounded | “A small mixed-method study found a context need and mixed results from a summary intervention.” |
| Optional Twitch companion content can attract interaction | Supported, contextual | “A large Dota esports Extension deployment observed about one-third direct use among telemetry-exposed users.” |
| ChatXPT reduces streamer attention or actions | Unproven | “ChatXPT will test whether its cue-to-sidequest loop reduces actions and active-attention time versus the manual Twitch workflow.” |
| ChatXPT increases meaningful participation | Unproven | “ChatXPT will compare accepted voting and lifecycle comprehension with native/manual baselines.” |
| ChatXPT improves viewer retention, growth, subscriptions, or revenue | Unsupported | Do not make the claim |
| ChatXPT has product-market fit | Unsupported | Do not make the claim |

## Research results versus internal product gates

The numerical gates in `LIVE-DIRECTOR-SECONDARY-RESEARCH.md` are team-authored falsification criteria. They are not literature results and must never be cited as external evidence.

| Internal gate | Purpose | Research status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At least 80% of surfaced Director Cues rated timely, supported, safe, and actionable | Reject imprecise cueing before viewer exposure | Proposed evaluation threshold; not validated |
| At least 90% of annotated high-focus intervals have no visible interruption | Reject phase awareness that still distracts | Proposed evaluation threshold; not validated |
| At least 30% lower median actions and active-attention time than manual Twitch poll/contest setup | Test the product's central value-add rather than rearranged primitives | Proposed comparative threshold; not validated |
| Catch-up improves correct current-state identification by at least 15 percentage points, reaches at least 80% correctness, and slows voting by no more than 10% | Reject a distracting or decorative viewer summary | Proposed experiment threshold; not validated |
| Same authoritative revision across Studio, two viewers, persistence, and OBS in every evaluation run | Prove the closed loop is technically coherent | Repository acceptance criterion; not market evidence |

## Implication for the scope decision

If the owner elects to proceed, `P0` should mean **first validation hypothesis**, not **proven value**:

- Test the existing exactly-three sidequest lifecycle as the core participation loop.
- Add only the minimum private cue/context needed to compare cue-to-vote effort with a manual Twitch workflow.
- Keep the OBS projection compressed and the Extension optional.
- Treat Catch-up and Session Brief as removable experiments.
- Do not activate generic chat, cohost, coaching, analytics, native desktop HUD, or direct-game-control scope.

No implementation pass is activated by this evidence ledger.
