# Live Director Evidence Matrix

**Owner:** Role 1 (`Dewflash`)

**Last updated:** 19 August 2026

**Method:** Secondary research and current-capability review only

**Status:** Evidence ledger complete for the current scope discussion; direct ChatXPT solution effect remains unproven

## Bottom line

The research supports a real but bounded problem: many creators operate with little audience interaction, while creators who do receive activity must divide attention between gameplay, performance, chat interpretation, and interaction setup. Useful feedback can then disappear into chat or remain disconnected from post-stream metrics. Late joiners can also lack context. Adjacent context and interaction systems can attract use, but their results are mixed.

It does **not** establish that ChatXPT's proposed Live Director loop reduces streamer effort, increases meaningful viewer participation, improves retention, or earns sustained adoption. ChatXPT is an evidence-informed product hypothesis. The first implementation priority, if accepted by the project owner, is the smallest experiment capable of proving or rejecting that hypothesis against existing Twitch and OBS workflows.

| Evidence level | Current conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| Pain exists | Supported for sparse-audience interaction, divided attention, audience-management cost, feedback loss, actionability gaps, chat overload, and some late-join context difficulty; prevalence in ChatXPT's exact Twitch game-streamer segment is not quantified |
| Adjacent mechanisms can work | Supported in bounded studies for context aids, interactive Extensions, polls, and audience influence; results are mixed and often observational |
| ChatXPT's exact solution works | **Not established**; no reviewed study evaluates the complete game-state + audience-signal + safe sidequest + Twitch vote + OBS payoff loop |
| ChatXPT improves retention, growth, or revenue | **Not established** |
| ChatXPT is technically plausible | Partly supported by bounded gameplay-signal and Extension deployments; ChatXPT still needs its own runtime and comparative evidence |

| Pain point | Direct streamer evidence and denominator | What is actually quantified | Current behaviour or workaround | ChatXPT response and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Never fabricate viewers, consensus, demand, or a guaranteed growth effect |
| Performing, playing, and reading chat compete for attention | Wohn and Freeman interviewed 25 Twitch streamers; Mok et al. observed five novices playing a turn-based game with and without a simulated audience | Wohn and Freeman report mechanisms, not a coded prevalence rate. Johnson and Woodcock document long hours and precariousness but do not publish a population prevalence | Full-time creators absorbed long schedules, audience/community work, safety burdens, and uncertainty; successful creators still relied on extensive self-taught labour | Treat low distraction, emergency pause, privacy, and bounded scope as safety requirements. ChatXPT is not a burnout, harassment, career, or income solution |

### Defensible pain statement

> Small and under-resourced livestream creators face two related engagement problems: many receive too little interaction to sustain the social experience they want, while those receiving bursts of activity must interpret and operationalise that activity while also playing and performing. Current chat, analytics, and off-platform community tools expose information, but research documents attention trade-offs, lost qualitative feedback, and limited actionable explanation. ChatXPT tests whether one source-labelled, phase-aware cue can turn a real audience signal into a safe, completed sidequest loop with less live operating effort.

The first sentence is supported across different platforms and samples; the final sentence is the product hypothesis. There is still no defensible statistic of the form “`X%` of small-to-medium Twitch game streamers have this exact problem,” and the repository must not invent one.

## Research results versus internal product gates

The numerical gates in `LIVE-DIRECTOR-SECONDARY-RESEARCH.md` are team-authored falsification criteria. They are not literature results and must never be cited as external evidence.

| Internal gate | Purpose | Research status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At least 80% of surfaced Director Cues rated timely, supported, safe, and actionable | Reject imprecise cueing before viewer exposure | Proposed evaluation threshold; not validated |
| At least 90% of annotated high-focus intervals have no visible interruption | Reject phase awareness that still distracts | Proposed evaluation threshold; not validated |
| At least 30% lower median actions and active-attention time than manual Twitch poll/contest setup | Test the product's central value-add rather than rearranged primitives | Proposed comparative threshold; not validated |
| Catch-up improves correct current-state identification by at least 15 percentage points, reaches at least 80% correctness, and slows voting by no more than 10% | Reject a distracting or decorative viewer summary | Proposed experiment threshold; not validated |
| Same authoritative revision across Studio, two viewers, persistence, and OBS in every evaluation run | Prove the closed loop is technically coherent | Repository acceptance criterion; not market evidence |

## Implication for the scope decision

If the owner elects to proceed, `P0` should mean **first validation hypothesis**, not **proven value**:

- Test the existing exactly-three sidequest lifecycle as the core participation loop.
- Add only the minimum private cue/context needed to compare cue-to-vote effort with a manual Twitch workflow.
- Keep the OBS projection compressed and the Extension optional.
- Treat Catch-up and Session Brief as removable experiments.
- Do not activate generic chat, cohost, coaching, analytics, native desktop HUD, or direct-game-control scope.

No implementation pass is activated by this evidence ledger.
